Proof of concept

Does AI with Intellect and Quantum make better decisions than AI alone?

Pick a real-world decision problem and see whether an AI model decides better when you add Intellect (your domain knowledge plus good reasoning habits) and a quantum search, compared with each piece on its own.

All 15 candidate use cases are here, marked by whether they passed the qualification test. Intellect now has two parts: domain knowledge, and the character layer from your intellect-config.yaml. Each run puts one decision problem through every stack and scores every answer against the exact optimum. The 20-trial proof repeats it on fresh instances, and the model test repeats it for nine Claude, OpenAI and open-weight models.

How it works

AIThe AI writes the problem as maths ?

A language model reads the problem and sets it up for a solver. It can misread details and miss rules that only your organisation knows.

INIntellect corrects it ?

Your rulebooks and data fill in what the model missed, and a character layer makes it flag its doubts and ask for missing rules.

QQuantum searches every answer ?

A simulated quantum computer explores all possible answers at once. An ordinary solver does the same job alongside it, as a fair check.

  1. Pick a problem in Setup, or create your own. The defaults are fine to start with.
  2. Press Run. It takes about a second.
  3. Read the verdict at the top of Results, then repeat on 20 problems to check it wasn't luck.
Everything runs in your browser. Your files and rules never leave it.
01 · What we're testing
Hypothesis 1 · the full stack

A model guided by Intellect (domain knowledge plus character), with quantum search over every possible answer, makes better decisions than the model alone, the model with quantum, or the model with Intellect, whichever model you start from.

Pass criteria: the full stack scores highest (ties count) and breaks no hard rule. Across trials or models it must be best or tied at least 70% of the time. Any good search can pass this test; Hypothesis 2 isolates quantum.

Supportedon 1 run · repeat on 20 problems to check
Hypothesis 2 · quantum itself ?

With the same Intellect, quantum search finds better answers than a classical solver that examines the same number of answers.

Pass criteria: quantum is ahead in more instances than it is behind, and ahead by at least 1 point on average. Caveat: QAOA tuning here reads exact probabilities from the simulator; real hardware would spend extra shots on tuning, which the classical solver's budget does not count, so a pass here overstates quantum.

    07 · Background: where quantum has really beaten classicalPublished results, October 2026

    These problems were solved on real quantum hardware where classical supercomputers could not match the result. They are too large to run in a browser, so this section reports the published experiments and lets you run a miniature of one. Every credible advantage so far is in simulating quantum physics, not in optimisation.

    Cross-validatedJuly 2026

    Materials driven by laser light

    IBM with Qedma

    Simulated how magnetisation oscillates in a material driven by a laser field, a building block for optical electronics.

    Hardware
    51-qubit IBM Heron r3, about 7 hours, with Qedma error mitigation
    Classical comparison
    Fugaku used about 700,000 CPU hours and did not reproduce the oscillations; an Nvidia H100 server ran out of memory. Matching it is estimated at 80,000 GPU servers for six months.
    Where AI + Intellect fit
    AI proposes which materials and drive frequencies to test; Intellect supplies the lab’s measured parameters and safety limits; quantum runs the dynamics no classical machine can.
    SourceRun the miniature
    Unrefuted for 8 months2026

    Disordered matter: catalysts and electrolytes

    IBM with Algorithmiq

    Simulated irregular, disordered quantum materials of the kind found in catalysts and battery electrolytes.

    Hardware
    Two IBM Heron chips, results consistent within error
    Classical comparison
    Classical methods ran into memory and time limits. Listed on the public Quantum Advantage Tracker for eight months with no classical reproduction, despite challenges from Flatiron Institute and others.
    Where AI + Intellect fit
    AI screens thousands of candidate electrolytes; Intellect removes ones that are toxic, costly or unstable from lab records; quantum simulates the few that remain.

    Preprint, not yet peer reviewedJuly 2026

    Verifiable hard-circuit sampling

    University of Chicago with IBM

    Ran a structured sampling benchmark with a built-in check that the computation was faithful.

    Hardware
    70 error-corrected logical qubits, 2,415 logical two-qubit gates, about 15 minutes
    Classical comparison
    Researchers report leading classical simulation methods face prohibitive runtimes; no specific supercomputer comparison is given.
    Where AI + Intellect fit
    None. This is a benchmark that proves the machine works, not a useful problem.

    Quantum Echoes for molecular structure

    Google Quantum AI with UC Berkeley

    Measured an “echo” signal (an out-of-time-order correlator) on the Willow chip, then applied the method to NMR data on molecules of 15 and 28 atoms.

    Hardware
    Google Willow superconducting chip
    Classical comparison
    Reported as 13,000 times faster than the best classical approach for the echo measurement. The molecules in the NMR demonstration were still small enough to model classically.
    Where AI + Intellect fit
    AI interprets NMR spectra; Intellect brings known chemistry of the compound family; quantum echoes reveal structure that classical NMR analysis misses.

    Glossary of terms
    AI layer
    A language model reads the problem and turns it into maths a solver can work on. It can misread relationships and miss rules, especially private ones it has never seen.
    Backend
    Which kind of quantum hardware to imitate. Error rates are typical public figures, not live data.
    Character layer
    Loaded from your intellect-config.yaml. Only honesty (calibration), curiosity (asking for missing rules) and openness (accepting correction) change results here, and their effects are modelled assumptions.
    Error mitigation
    Software techniques that partly undo hardware noise after measuring.
    Fidelity
    How much of the circuit survives hardware noise. 1.00 is perfect; lower means more shots come out as random answers.
    Hard rule
    A rule that must never be broken, such as two ambulance bases that share one overloaded hospital. Breaking one makes the answer unusable.
    Instance
    One randomly generated version of the problem. New instance draws another one with different numbers.
    Intellect
    What sits on top of the model: domain knowledge (rulebooks, data, your files) and a character layer (flagging doubts, asking for missing rules, accepting correction).
    Layers (p)
    How many rounds the quantum algorithm runs. More rounds can steer it better but add gates, and gates add noise.
    Ordinary (classical) solver
    A normal computer search, simulated annealing, allowed to check as many answers as the quantum computer takes shots. If it does as well, quantum added nothing on that problem.
    QAOA
    Quantum Approximate Optimisation Algorithm. It alternates a cost step and a mixing step so that low-cost answers become more likely.
    Quantum layer
    A quantum computer, simulated in your browser, that holds all possible answers at once and uses interference to make good ones more likely to be measured.
    Qubit
    A quantum bit. Here each qubit is one yes-or-no decision, so 8 qubits cover 256 possible answers.
    Repeat on 20 problems
    Runs the whole experiment on 20 fresh versions of the problem, so a single lucky run cannot decide the verdict.
    Score
    Every answer is scored as a percentage of the best possible answer, which is found here by checking every option. An answer that breaks a hard rule scores 0.
    Shots
    How many times the quantum circuit is run and measured. Each shot gives one candidate answer.

    Circuits run as exact statevector simulations in your browser, with a noise model for the chosen hardware class. Backend error rates are representative public figures for each class, not live calibration data. Model reasoning and rule-recall factors are illustrative, scaled from each model's public standing; the simulator does not call any model API. In the complex scenarios, the hard rules are treated as private data that no public model has seen; how much that holds for your own data is the assumption a real pilot would test.

    At 10 qubits or fewer a classical solver also finds the exact optimum, which is why it can be shown here as the 100% reference. The simulator shows how the layers combine; the scale where quantum hardware beats classical search is far beyond what a browser can emulate.

    The character layer is modelled, not measured. Calibration removes the model's largest formulation errors in proportion to its strength (filled from knowledge when a pack or file is connected, otherwise left blank); curiosity raises the share of hard rules captured; openness sets how much of an expert correction is applied. Directness, humility, wit and practical wisdom in the config have no measurable effect on a formulation here, so they are not modelled. Testing with real model calls is what a pilot would add.

    No stack sees the true rules when it decides. Quantum and classical searches each pick the best measured or visited answer under their own formulation; only the scoring uses the true rules and the exact optimum.

    Uploaded files are parsed only in this page. The simulator reads each file's domain, the scenario items it mentions and the rules it states, and feeds that into the Intellect layer.
